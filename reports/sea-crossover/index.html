SEA vs CM candidate supply for one-shot ECPP: the crossover, measured

Branch sea-crossover. Prover experiment comparing the two candidate-order supplies for n⁴-smooth one-shot certificates: the CM route (dscan discriminant scan + adaptive smoothness ladder + class polynomial, i.e. oneshot) versus SEA sampling (random Montgomery curves, PARI ellap point counts, same smoothness gate, ecpp/sea_supply.py). Asymptotically CM is p1/4+o(1) (quadratic scan: Θ(B) scanned for Θ(√B) usable discriminants, and the winner's class polynomial of degree h = Θ(√B) is a second quadratic wall), while SEA sampling is p1/8+o(1) (polynomial cost per candidate, so the total is just the inverse winner density). The question here: where is the practical crossover?

Setup

  • Same prime per size (pbits/seed=1 convention), same machine per race: aws-spot-15 (c7a.48xlarge, 192 Zen 4 cores, 384 GiB), PARI/GP 2.15.4; 256-bit legs ran on the dev box (Ryzen AI Max 395, 16 cores).
  • CM route: oneshot p=<p> threads=192 B=2e12 (adaptive ladder, all knobs default).
  • SEA route: sea_supply.py p=<p> workers=188 — parallel gp workers sample Montgomery coefficients A (both twists per point count: N = p+1∓t), batches gated through smoothtest gate against the same n⁴ bound and window as oneshot; winners assembled into voneshot-format tuples (p, A, x0, m, q₁..qₖ) with voneshot.verify() as the acceptance oracle.
  • Every certificate in the table was verified by the challenge's voneshot.py (True).

SEA's per-candidate cost is flat by construction (one point count per two candidates; measured in-session ellap 14.2 s at 384, 20.2 s at 416, 27.3 s at 448 per curve per worker on the c7a). CM's is an average over a mostly-linear regime (segment builds and batched smooth tests) plus the quadratic scan and the winner's class polynomial, which grow with the pool; at these sizes the quadratic terms are only beginning to bind, which is why CM's average per-candidate cost is still at or below SEA's flat rate.

Reading

On expected cost, CM stayed ahead at every measured size; the plain-SEA crossover extrapolates to roughly 480–512 bits. Individual walls flip winners on density luck (SEA's 3.8× win at 384 came from a lucky 16k-candidate draw; CM's 416 win partly from a lucky small-|D| winner), but the per-candidate costs are unambiguous: SEA pays a flat 7–14 core-s per candidate order (rising with n as SEA slows), while CM's average was still 2–4.5 core-s at pools up to 3×10⁵ candidates — below SEA's flat rate at every measured point. CM's quadratic terms are only beginning to bind at 448 (scan B = 1.3×10¹¹, winner class polynomial h ≈ 1.7×10⁵); extrapolating both curves, SEA's flat rate undercuts CM's rising average once expected pools reach ~10⁶ candidates — around 480–512 bits for typical density draws, sooner on unlucky ones — close to the pre-race model estimate (~410–430) but shifted up because the measured CM implementation (cached segments, parallel classpoly) is cheaper than the model priced. Three caveats worth recording: (i) this SEA baseline implements none of the proposed accelerations (small-ℓ stage-1 gate, batch tail, torsion family bias — the first two need SEA internals that PARI does not expose; modeled combined gain ~×3–5, which would pull the crossover down toward ~350–380 bits); (ii) the CM route's winner class polynomial is its structural wall — at 448 the in-race |D| already reached 1.3×10¹¹, and the HD size/time curve (Θ(N²) in the candidate count, like the scan) is what caps pure-CM one-shots near ~500 bits regardless of patience; (iii) SEA's per-curve cost is pure Schoof–Elkies–Atkin in a stock gp 2.15.4 — evaluated-Φℓ methods and Magma's SEA would shave constants. Net: below ~380 bits use the scan; above ~450 the scan's economics fail while SEA sampling continues to scale as p1/8+o(1) until the density itself becomes the wall (~10⁹ candidates around 640 bits).

The 448 SEA leg was stopped by hand at 20 035 s (2.8× the CM wall, 275 600 candidates tested without a winner, both routes' density draws censored-consistent) to shut the spot meter off — the per-candidate ratio at 448 (13.7 vs 4.5 core-s) was already pinned.
