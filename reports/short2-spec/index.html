Short ECPP v2: the radical-capped revision

2026-08-21 · AVS + Claude (Fable 5) · OneShotFastECPP, branch short-ecpp. Working note for the August 2026 revision of the ShortPrimalityProofs format; design discussion in the session of 2026-08-19–21 (see reports/ecpp-varieties/HANDOFF.md) and the write-up reports/ecpp-varieties/ecpp-varieties.pdf (Definition 6.2, Proposition 6.4).

The revision

A certificate is, as before, the flat sequence (p_0, A_0, x_0, o_0, A_1, x_1, o_1, ..., A_k, x_k, o_k) with n = ceil(log2 p_0) fixed for the whole chain, each level working modulo p_i with o_i = m_i · p_{i+1}. The revision changes three clauses, with B = floor(n²/log₂ n):

  1. Smoothness. m_i is the B-smooth part of o_i (every prime of m_i is ≤ B; no prime of o_i may lie in (B, n²]).
  2. Radical cap. floor(log₂ rad(m_i)) < n/log₂ n, where rad(m) is the product of the distinct primes of m.
  3. Explicit floor. p_{i+1} = 1 (terminal) or n² < p_{i+1} and p_{i+1}² < p_i (roughness now guarantees only p_{i+1} > B, so the floor is stated).

Window (L_i < o_i < r_i L_i, r_i the least prime of m_i), minimality, point-order semantics, and the payload are unchanged. Every v2 certificate is valid under the original definition (the new conditions only restrict), and existence for every prime p_0 ≥ 5 is untouched: a 2-power filler has radical 2.

Why: verification becomes O(n² log n), self-contained

The original format verifies in O(n²(log n)²) worst case (its k = 0 stratum is the unrestricted-filler restricted one-shot), and reaching O(n² log n) on published certificates needs the product tree of the primes to n² — a per-n table costing Θ(n²(log n)²) to build. The two caps remove both obstacles at once:

  • The primorial of B has Θ(n²/log n) bits, so it is built per certificate in O(n² log n) — no table.
  • gcd(P_B mod o_i, o_i) = rad(m_i) exactly, an integer of fewer than n/log₂ n bits whose prime factors are ≤ B; the radicals of the whole chain are factored within the budget (batched against the primorial tree, or per-level Pollard–Strassen; the reference verifier simply trial-divides — coarsest asymptotically, simplest, and dominant only far beyond feasible sizes).
  • The radical cap bounds the order-exactness tree by (n/log₂ n)·ceil(log₂ k) < n ladder bits per level, so the elliptic work is O(Σ b_i M(b_i)) = O(n² log n), about 5× Pomerance's constant.

Proposition. A v2 certificate for an n-bit p_0 is verified in O(n² log n) bit operations and O(n²) bits of memory, worst case over certificates, with no precomputation of any kind.

The finding analysis is untouched: the caps constrain only window completion, a polynomial factor. 74% of the 120 published levels satisfied both caps incidentally (a per-level acceptance factor of ≈1.35 for a finder that filters).

Migration of the published table

Of the 31 published chains (certs.csv, upstream commit 0408b2b): 10 already comply (c = 70, 150, 160, 180, 200, 250, 260, 270, 290, 310) and are kept byte-identical; 21 were re-found from their first non-compliant level (the compliant prefix survives; the level moduli above the repair point are unchanged). Every repaired chain is verified under both the v2 and the original definitions.

Upstream adoption checklist (ShortPrimalityProofs)

  2. Replace vsmallECPP.py by (a renamed) vshort2.py, or ship both during a transition — the old verifier accepts every v2 certificate but does not enforce the caps.
  3. Replace certs.csv by the migrated table.
  4. Replace short.gp by short2.gp (same toy style, same knobs).
  5. Regenerate the exhaustive small-p data (short8all.txt): the v2 enumeration below 28 is smaller (the caps thin the admissible orders; e.g. for p = 251 the terminal orders are 24, 25, 27, 32, 36, 49). Existence for every prime p ≥ 5 still holds (2-power fillers).
