Short ECPP records: nextprime(10c) for c = 210, …, 310

Headline. Every least prime above 10c for c = 210, 220, …, 310 now has a verified short ECPP certificate (ShortPrimalityProofs format: O(n)-bit certificates, quasi-quadratic verification), extending the previous frontier of 10200. The 1030-bit prime 10310+733 exceeds the 1025-bit AKS example of Bernstein — to our knowledge the largest prime with an AKS-style proof — while its certificate is 5,854 bits (≈732 bytes) and verifies in about a second. All certificates were produced in a single day (2026-08-18) by a CM-method prover built on the OneShotFastECPP toolchain, each verified independently twice (vsmallECPP.py and a PARI ellmul cross-check of every level).

Method

A short ECPP level for a modulus p needs a curve order N = c·m·q where only m (n²-smooth, n fixed at the top of the chain) and the prime q < √p are exhibited — the cofactor c is never factored. Per level:

  1. Candidate supply: the dscan Cornacchia scan over fundamental discriminants (factor base of precomputed square roots; the new maxfb option bounds the factor base at 108, trading ~15% of usable density for a Tonelli pass that costs seconds instead of hours, which is what makes 105-candidate pools affordable at 1030 bits). Each solvable D yields two orders p+1±t; supply cost ≈0.1–1 core-s per candidate.
  2. Batched smooth stripping (Bernstein remainder trees against a cached primorial of all primes ≤ 232): splits N = s·(middles)·T at ≈0.5 core-s per candidate. The window rule L < m·q < r(m)·L makes the whole smooth part s act as coverage: a prime ℓ | s alone covers the entire range q ∈ (L/ℓ, L), so P(win) ∝ ln s — the key softening versus one-shot certificates.
  3. ECM peeling of the rough tails (staged gmp-ecm, B1 = 2K → 3M): >90% of all cost. Scheduling evolved during the campaign into a Bayesian index policy: rank by ln(ln s) − 0.03·(tail bits above √p) − 0.01·(failed curves at the tier), round-robin sub-sweeps (every disc gets attempt k before any disc gets k+1), conditional duplicate slots when the pool narrows, and an economic widen trigger — extend the discriminant bound when the marginal admitted index drops below a fresh draw's median, since a fresh candidate costs 30–300× less than a deep ECM visit.
  4. Winner construction: HD by classpoly (best class invariant, parallel ECRT), Montgomery A from the u-cubic, 2-volcano floor descent when p ≡ 3 (mod 4) requires a cyclic 2-Sylow, point of exact order m·q by the x-only ladder; levels below 135 bits finish via short.gp (SEA).

CM versus SEA supply

Measured at 1030 bits on the driver box: one PARI ellap point count = 3,749 s (≈31 core-min per candidate order), versus ≈0.1–1 core-s per candidate from the bounded-factor-base scan — a >103× supply-cost gap (698 bits: 413 s/curve, same conclusion). Because the short-ECPP acceptance criterion is so much softer than the one-shot n4-smooth condition, candidate demand stays in the 104–105 range where the scan's quadratic-in-demand costs never bind; extrapolating both cost curves puts the SEA/CM crossover for short ECPP near 2000 bits — effectively past the feasibility horizon of the shared ECM stage — versus ≈500 bits measured for one-shot certificates in the July race.
